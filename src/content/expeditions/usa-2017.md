The 21 August 2017 eclipse crossed the United States on an unbroken land path of some three thousand miles. The Sherpas occupied five sites between Oregon and Nebraska, repeating a core instrument suite so that cloud at one location would not erase the experiment.

The scientific target was a corona observed across a solar cycle of eclipse data: electron temperature distributions, ion charge-state evolution, and the thermodynamics of any CME fronts that happened to be in the field. Multi-wavelength imaging of coronal forbidden lines was paired with high-resolution white light.

It remains one of the most ambitious coordinated eclipse campaigns the team has run — a reminder that totality is brief, but the path can be used as a laboratory if enough people are willing to carry the instruments.
